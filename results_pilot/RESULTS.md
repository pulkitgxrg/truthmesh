# Results (pilot)

**Scope.** These are the only results that exist: a pilot on 50 HotpotQA *validation* questions (distractor setting), one run per system (U = 1), comparing CV-SM with B1 (single agent) and B5 (multi-agent debate). All agents used `openai/gpt-4.1-nano` (one model family). CV-SM ran with the configuration in `configs/pilot_v2.yaml` (untuned parameters, closed-book drafts, ρ̂ estimated on 50 disjoint validation questions). B2, B3, B4, B6, the ablations A1–A8, the k and τ sweeps, FEVER, TruthfulQA and HaluEval were **not run**, and no human annotation was done. Intervals are 95% bootstrap intervals over queries (10,000 resamples). Every number is read from `results_pilot/paper_numbers.json`, generated by `scripts/pilot_results.py` from the logged runs.

## Hypotheses

- **H1 — not supported (pilot).** CV-SM did not have a lower hallucination rate than debate: HR CV-SM 18.8 [0.0, 40.0]% vs B5 11.6 [5.4, 18.9]% (ΔHR +7.2 pp, 95% CI [-12.0, +26.0], Holm-adjusted p = 0.52). Against B1 (HR 36.1 [26.4, 46.3]%), ΔHR = -17.4 pp [-41.1, +5.0], Holm-adjusted p = 0.24. CV-SM answered only 16.0 [8.0, 26.0]% of questions (floor u_min = 80%), so its HR rests on 2.0 claims per answer from 8 answers. Exact match: CV-SM 0.12 [0.04, 0.22], B5 0.52 [0.38, 0.66], B1 0.38 [0.24, 0.52]; McNemar (Holm) CV-SM vs B5 p = 2.2e-05, vs B1 p = 2.3e-03 (CV-SM worse). Only 2 of the 6 baselines were compared.
- **H2 — inconclusive (not tested).** B6 (unguarded shared memory) was not run. For reference, mean PD was 0.40 [0.29, 0.54] for B5 and 0.17 [0.10, 0.25] for CV-SM.
- **H3 — inconclusive (not tested).** Ablation A1 (−R1) was not run. Descriptively, CLR was 80.0 [40.0, 100.0]% for CV-SM and 60.5 [45.5, 75.0]% for B5.
- **H4 — inconclusive (not tested).** B6 was not run. CV-SM's MCR(t) slope was +0.0072 per write, 95% CI [-0.0187, +0.0311] (no detectable growth), which is consistent with half of H4 only.
- **H5 — inconclusive (not tested).** The k ∈ {2, 5} runs were not made; only k = 3 cost is available (Table VIII).

## Other findings

- **Over-blocking.** CV-SM blocked 63.8 [53.7, 73.1]% of true internal claims (FRR) and 91.5 [85.6, 96.1]% of false ones (VSR); escape rate 2.2 [0.3, 5.4]% vs 49.6 [38.9, 59.2]% for B5. What CV-SM wrote to memory was mostly correct: precision Corroborated 91.2% [82.4, 100.0] (n=34), Resolved 90.0% [70.0, 100.0] (n=10), Singleton-grounded 84.8% [78.6, 90.3] (n=145).
- **Correlated agents.** ρ̂ = 0.80, 95% CI [0.62, 0.94], for three role prompts on one base model; with ρ̂ this high, agreement among all three agents adds little confidence under Eq. (11).
- **NLI false vetoes.** With `cross-encoder/nli-deberta-v3-base` as the system NLI (first pilot run), 232 clusters had G ≤ −0.9. In a seeded sample of 60, `ling-wanli` judged 31 contradictions, 24 neutral and 4 entailments. Switching the system NLI raised coverage from 10% to 16% and lowered FRR from 82.5% to 63.8%.
- **Calibration.** CV-SM ECE 0.290 [0.213, 0.369], Brier 0.278 [0.229, 0.330].
- **Cost per query (list price, gpt-4.1-nano).** CV-SM $0.00228 (30.7 LLM calls, 231 NLI calls, median latency 124 s); B5 $0.00277 (21.2 calls); B1 $0.00004.
- **Power.** Detecting a 5-point HR difference at power 0.8 would need n ≈ 1529 (CV-SM vs B5) to 2225 (vs B1) queries at α = 0.05, given the pilot variance. The pilot (n = 50) is far from this.
